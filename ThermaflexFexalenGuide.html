Engineering Warning

Failing to distinguish between Polybutene-1 (PB-1) and Cross-linked Polyethylene (PEX) is a critical error. PB-1 offers significantly higher creep resistance and stress-crack resistance at elevated temperatures. If your network design specifies high continuous loads, substituting Flexalen for generic PEX will drastically reduce the safety factor of your entire installation.

What is Thermaflex Flexalen and Why Does It Matter?

Thermaflex Flexalen is a premium range of flexible, pre-insulated plastic piping systems designed for the distribution of heating, cooling, and potable water. But to understand why it outperforms virtually every competitor, we must look at the specific materials utilized in its tri-layer construction.

First, the Carrier Pipe. Unlike standard district heating pipes that use rigid steel or PEX, Flexalen utilizes Polybutene-1 (PB-1). PB-1 is the holy grail of polyolefins for pressurized hot water. It possesses the lowest thermal expansion, the highest flexibility, and the greatest resistance to creep (deformation under long-term stress) of any plastic piping material. Crucially, PB-1 can be homogeneously welded, meaning the pipe and the fitting fuse into one identical material, eliminating mechanical failure points entirely.

Second, the Insulation. Traditional systems use Polyurethane (PUR) foam. While PUR is an excellent insulator when dry, it acts like a sponge if the outer casing is breached, absorbing groundwater, ruining thermal efficiency, and rotting the core pipe. Flexalen uses a proprietary closed-cell polyolefin foam. It is entirely water-repellent (hydrophobic). Even if an excavator punctures the outer casing, the insulation will not absorb water, preserving the system's exact thermal performance.

The Flexalen Network Deployment Strategy

Deploying a supply of Thermaflex Flexalen Pipes requires a paradigm shift from traditional steel pipe laying. Adhere to this 5-phase protocol to guarantee a 50-year operational lifecycle.

01

Thermal Load & Pressure Analysis

Before breaking ground, calculate your exact continuous and peak loads. While PB-1 is incredibly resilient, network design must respect the pressure/temperature curves. For standard networks (80°C / 8 Bar), Flexalen 600 is optimal. For massive transmission lines, specify Flexalen PU. Under-sizing the carrier pipe will increase fluid velocity, causing unnecessary pump strain and potential acoustic issues.

02

Trench Optimization & Routing

Because Flexalen is highly flexible, you do not need wide trenches to accommodate hot-works welding or rigid 90-degree elbows. Map the route to sweep around existing underground utilities (gas, telecom) using the natural bending radius of the pipe. Blind the trench bottom with 100mm of sharp sand to prevent point-loading on the corrugated casing from sharp rocks.

03

Homogeneous Joint Preparation

If not using factory prefabrications, jointing requires absolute cleanliness. Polybutene-1 develops a microscopic oxidation layer when exposed to air. You MUST mechanically scrape this layer off the pipe ends before using the electrofusion or socket welding tools. Wiping it is insufficient. Clean the scraped area with isopropyl alcohol. Failure to scrape is the #1 cause of hydrostatic test failure.

04

Automated Fusion & Hydrostatic Testing

Utilize the Flexalen microprocessor-controlled welding box. Scan the barcode on the fitting to initiate the precise heating and cooling cycle. Allow the joint to cool completely untouched. Before insulating the joints or backfilling the trench, execute a hydrostatic pressure test (typically 1.5x operating pressure) for a minimum of two hours to verify absolute integrity.

05

Hermetic Sealing & Backfill

Once tested, apply the shrinkable EPDM end-caps at building entries to prevent moisture from entering the foam. Fit the insulation shells over the welded joints and seal them permanently. Backfill the trench using sand in the immediate pipe zone, lay down metallic tracer wire and warning tape, and complete the final topsoil or asphalt reinstatement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between PB-1 and PEX for district heating?
Cross-linked Polyethylene (PEX) is a common, inexpensive plastic used in many standard pipes. However, Polybutene-1 (PB-1), utilized in Thermaflex Flexalen Pre Insulated Pipes, is fundamentally superior for high-stress thermal applications. PB-1 has a higher resistance to creep (stretching over time under pressure) and retains much higher flexibility. Crucially, PB-1 can be homogeneously welded using heat fusion to create seamless joints. PEX cannot be welded—it requires mechanical brass or steel compression fittings, which introduce severe points of failure, flow restriction, and corrosion risk underground.
Is Flexalen worth the premium over standard PUR-insulated steel pipes?
Absolutely. While the initial material cost per meter might appear higher than generic steel, the *installed* lifecycle cost is drastically lower. Flexalen eliminates the need for expensive X-ray tested steel welding, narrows trench widths, and removes the need for expansion loops. Furthermore, traditional open-cell PUR foam absorbs water if the casing leaks, destroying insulation values. Flexalen's closed-cell polyolefin foam is entirely hydrophobic, guaranteeing its thermal performance for 50+ years regardless of groundwater conditions.
How do I join Flexalen pipes together?
Flexalen PB-1 carrier pipes are joined using homogeneous thermal fusion. For smaller diameters, this is done via socket fusion (melting the outside of the pipe and the inside of the fitting and pushing them together). For larger mains, electrofusion is used. A specialized fitting containing heating wire is placed over the pipes, and an automated computer applies precise voltage to melt the polymers together. The resulting joint is molecularly identical to the pipe itself, providing unparalleled strength.
Can Flexalen be used for cooling and chilled water networks?
Yes, exceptionally well. Flexalen PHC (Pre-insulated Heating and Cooling) is specifically tailored for this. When transporting chilled water (e.g., 6°C), standard pipes suffer from heavy surface condensation, causing structural water damage in ceilings and shafts. The continuous, closed-cell foam insulation of Flexalen perfectly acts as a vapor barrier, entirely preventing condensation while maintaining the chilled water temperature over long internal runs.
What does Cradle to Cradle Certified mean for my project?
Thermaflex Flexalen holds Cradle to Cradle (C2C) Silver certification. This is the global standard for products that are safe, circular, and responsibly made. It proves that the Polybutene-1 and Polyolefin foams contain no toxic off-gassing chemicals, are manufactured using renewable energy, and are 100% recyclable at the end of their 50-year life. Using C2C certified Flexalen adds massive points to green building standards like LEED, BREEAM, and DGNB, making it vital for sustainable municipal projects.
How deep do Thermaflex pipes need to be buried?
Generally, Flexalen pipes should be buried at a minimum depth of 600mm to 800mm (from the top of the pipe casing to the surface) to protect against heavy surface loads (like traffic) and to remain beneath the frost line. Because the system is so highly insulated, frost protection is exceptional, but adhering to local civil engineering codes regarding traffic loading on the corrugated HDPE casing is mandatory. A 100mm sand surround is also required to prevent rock puncture.
Can I transition from Flexalen PB-1 to existing copper or steel systems?
Yes. Thermaflex provides a comprehensive range of transition fittings. These specialized accessories feature a PB-1 weldable tail on one end (to fuse directly into the Flexalen network) and a threaded or flanged brass/steel connection on the other. This allows you to seamlessly integrate the modern polymer network into legacy plant room manifolds, pumps, or existing building steelwork without introducing galvanic corrosion risks into the plastic side of the system.
